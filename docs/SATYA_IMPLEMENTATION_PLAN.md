## 1. Product definition

**Satya reconstructs what a business ordered, received, was billed for, and paid—then explains discrepancies with evidence and a safe next step.**

Business-friendly tagline: **“Know what you actually owe, and why.”**

Technical characterization: an evidence-led, line-level purchase-to-payment reconciliation and investigation workspace.

It is NOT:

- A full ERP, warehouse management system, or general ledger.
- An autonomous accountant or legal determination of liability.
- A fraud detector that labels suppliers dishonest based on anomalies.
- A chatbot that guesses financial balances from documents.
- A graph visualization whose connections are generated by an LLM.
- A generic invoice OCR demo with a dashboard attached.

### 1.1 Explicit problem-statement coverage

| Requirement | Concrete implementation | Visible proof |
|---|---|---|
| Match imperfect keys/descriptions/quantities/dates | Candidate blocking, aliases, semantic retrieval, trained pair scorer, constrained allocation | Match comparison drawer showing alternatives and feature evidence |
| Duplicate charges | Distinguish duplicate upload, repeated representation of one invoice, and separate suspected duplicate billing | Duplicate cluster, separate source rows, human confirmation path |
| Partial deliveries | Line-level cumulative receipt/acceptance ledger with due dates | Ordered/received/accepted/invoiced progress bars |
| Omitted records | Missing-evidence checks gated by source coverage and policy | Missing evidence card, not fabricated transaction |
| Currency/amount mismatches | Decimal arithmetic, currency guards, price/tax/freight checks | Formula trace and original currency |
| Confidence and evidence trails | Extraction quality, calibrated linkage, rule certainty, provenance, decision history | Clickable source row/page and confidence breakdown |
| Four operational actions | Approve, Request Clarification, Escalate, Place on Hold | Action panel with amount, owner, prerequisite, and draft |
| Ambiguous entries | Alternatives, unallocated amounts, bounded scenarios, abstention | “Needs evidence” queue and explanation of what resolves it |
| Required target scenario | Partial PO, duplicate invoices, split payments | Reproducible showcase in section 6 |

### 1.2 What makes the demonstration distinctive

The novelty is the combination and inspectability, not a claim that no competitor has these features.

1. **Conservation ledger:** one receipt unit and one payment unit cannot be silently spent twice.
2. **Evidence clock:** compare what was known on a past date with what arrived later.
3. **Ambiguity budget:** show how much money remains uncertain and which document could resolve it.
4. **Competing explanations:** “This transfer might settle invoice A or invoice B” with reasons and feasible balances.
5. **Resolution preview:** simulate a credit note, receipt, or reviewer allocation and show a before/after balance without changing live state.
6. **Evidence-linked copilot:** every answer can open the exact case, source field, and Neo4j path used.
7. **Business-oriented prioritization:** separate money awaiting legitimate delivery from suspected duplicate exposure and already-paid loss.
8. **Replayable audit packet:** the same snapshot, rules, and model version reproduce the finding without an LLM.

### 1.3 Scope tiers

**CORE / MUST:** structured multi-source imports, persistent records, line matching, duplicate/receipt/amount/payment rules, exact balances, evidence, uncertainty, four actions, case UI, read-only copilot, Neo4j case graph, audit, seeded demo, tests.

**DIFFERENTIATORS / SHOULD:** trained pair linkage, semantic descriptions, global allocation, as-of replay, what-if preview, confidence calibration report, supplier history, public-data evidence panel.

**STRETCH:** scanned-document OCR/Docling, cross-encoder fine-tuning, active-learning retraining, survival estimates for late records, process mining, outbound sales reconciliation, production ERP connectors.

Do not build all stretch features before the core target scenario works.

## 2. Business workflow, explained simply

### 2.1 Simulated company

Use **Satya Equipment Distribution**, a fictional mid-sized distributor buying equipment from suppliers and supplying customers in three sectors:

- Industrial maintenance: pumps, motors, bearings, valves.
- Electrical/building operations: switches, meters, cables, power supplies.
- Laboratory operations: balances, centrifuges, benchtop instruments, accessories.

Use fictional vendor and customer names. Prices, tax rates, lead times, and error rates are demo assumptions unless explicitly sourced. Do not imply a relationship to a real company.

The initial app observes **the distributor as buyer**, investigating inbound supplier purchases. Downstream customer sales are business context only, not included in payable balances.

Why this setting works:

- Similar descriptions can refer to different models or specifications.
- Deliveries can be split across warehouses and dates.
- A box/pair/set is not automatically the same as a single unit.
- Some equipment requires inspection before acceptance.
- Supplier invoices and bank transfers often arrive through different systems.

### 2.2 Workflow and roles

1. A department requests equipment: purchase requisition, usually an internal request.
2. Procurement approves a purchase order (PO): buyer's agreed request specifying supplier, items, quantities, price, delivery schedule, and terms.
3. Supplier confirms the order; may issue a **pro forma invoice (PI)** for a quotation or advance request.
4. Supplier dispatches goods: shipment notice/delivery note/carrier evidence.
5. Warehouse records a goods receipt note (GRN): what the buyer actually received.
6. Inspection may accept or reject units: four-way matching uses accepted quantity, not just physically received quantity.
7. Supplier sends the commercial invoice: a request for payment with line amounts, taxes/charges, references, and due date.
8. Accounts payable checks it against order and receipt/acceptance evidence, including earlier invoices against the same order.
9. A finance reviewer approves, queries, holds, or escalates an exception.
10. Treasury schedules payment. A bank transfer confirms money movement; remittance advice describes what it is meant to settle.
11. Returns, credit notes, discounts, payment reversals, and amended orders change the state.
12. Accounts payable reconciles statements and closes an obligation when evidence and settlement support closure.

The order is not universally chronological. A commercial invoice may arrive before the goods. Advance payments may precede receipts. Do not flag those as errors without the applicable contract policy.

### 2.3 Glossary

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| PO | What the buyer ordered under agreed terms | Proof of receipt or payment |
| PI / pro forma | Preliminary commercial document, often quotation/advance request | A final invoice automatically increasing AP |
| Commercial invoice | Supplier's charge/request for payment | Proof that the charged goods were received |
| GRN | Buyer's record of physically received goods | Supplier dispatch notice |
| Acceptance/inspection | Goods passed buyer checks | Quantity delivered to loading bay |
| Remittance advice | Intended invoice allocation of a payment | Independently verified bank settlement |
| Bank settlement | Money movement recorded as settled | Proof of the intended invoice allocation |
| Credit note | Supplier-recognized reduction/correction of a charge | Cash refund or automatic payment reversal |
| Return | Physical goods sent back | Automatic credit note |
| AP | Accounts payable: supplier obligations recorded by buyer | Cash balance or customer receivables |
| UOM | Unit of measure: each, box, metre, kg, set | Arbitrary text synonym |
| Open quantity | Ordered quantity not yet fulfilled/cancelled | Automatically an overdue shortage |
| Match | Records likely refer to the same commercial event/item | Approval to pay |
| Reconciliation | Explain and balance related records | Edit source documents to make them agree |
| Hold | Internal control against further approval/payment | Reversing a payment already made |
| Snapshot | Reproducible calculation with specific inputs and rules | Unversioned dashboard cache |

### 2.4 Matching policies

- Two-way: approved order/contract and invoice; appropriate for defined services or where receipt is not required.
- Three-way: order, buyer goods receipt, invoice.
- Four-way: order, goods receipt, inspection acceptance, invoice.
- The distinction is established in current Oracle procurement documentation [R1] and Dynamics documentation [R2].
- Advance-payment policy must be explicit; it can allow payment before delivery without pretending goods were received.
- Consignment, blanket contracts, milestone billing, and complex service procurement are initially recognized as special flows and routed to review, not forced into the equipment three-way rules.

## 3. Corrections to the initial concept

### 3.1 Correct but incomplete ideas

MongoDB + Neo4j + a chatbot is a useful foundation. However:

- MongoDB persistence alone does not provide accounting correctness; the schema and conservation invariants do.
- Neo4j cannot independently establish truth. It projects evidence, matches, decisions, and allocations whose status must remain visible.
- “All business history” is not an achievable safe context window. Query authorized data with bounded tools, aggregation, retrieval, and citations.
- Matching similar invoice totals is not enough. Repeated monthly orders can legitimately have identical totals.
- Equipment similarity must preserve specifications: a 5 kW motor and a 7.5 kW motor are not interchangeable because embeddings are close.
- Partial delivery is often normal. It becomes a discrepancy when billing/policy/accepted quantity or due-date conditions are violated.
- A missing document may reflect an incomplete export, not a missing business event.
- A bank fee, authorized discount, tax withholding, or FX conversion may explain a payment difference; only supporting evidence permits that classification.

### 3.2 Unnecessary complexity to avoid

Do not preserve old federated-learning, differential-privacy, fraud-ring, PSI/VFL, or PaySim functionality in the Satya navigation. It solves a different problem.

Do not train a GNN, foundation model, or reinforcement-learning agent just to appear advanced. The strongest core is:

**learned linkage + explicit constraints + exact arithmetic + evidence + uncertainty + usable resolution.**

### 3.3 Honest claim boundaries

Say “research-backed hybrid demonstration,” not “real company transaction data,” for constructed equipment transactions.

Say “suspected duplicate,” not “fraud,” unless a reviewer establishes duplicate billing; even then intent is not established.

Say “supported payable/eligible for review under policy,” not “legally owed,” without accounting/legal review.

Say “potential duplicate exposure,” not “money saved,” before a completed resolution establishes prevented/recovered value.

### 5.4 BPI integration contract

- Preserve the original event log and anonymized values.
- Use a streaming XES parser with safe XML settings; do not load an enormous XML tree by default.
- Discover the actual case/event keys and activity semantics before mapping.
- Group by purchasing document and item; preserve subsidiary/source/vendor distinctions.
- Select a documented stratified subset of complete cases, not a random sample of isolated events.
- Include all events from each selected case unless demonstrating an explicitly constructed omission.
- Preserve original identifiers under a `bpi2019:` namespace.
- Do not invent item quantities, invoice numbers, currencies, taxes, acceptance records, or bank transactions missing from the log.
- Monetary values are anonymized by a zero-preserving linear transformation. Display “anonymized value units” unless the source explicitly supports a currency interpretation.
- Clearing an invoice event is source evidence of clearing, not a verified independent bank transfer.
- Understand cumulative versus incremental value fields through profiling; blindly summing cumulative events double-counts.
- Exclude reversed/cancelled events using mapped semantics, with reversal references where supported.
- Public-data page shows event counts, source date, assumptions, supported fields, and unsupported fields.
- Separate process-flow evaluation from constructed line-level/payment-allocation evaluation.

### 5.5 Constructed business generation

Minimum showcase: 12 scenario families, at least 3 variants each, roughly 100–250 records. This is a development target, not an existing artifact.

Evaluation scale target: 2,000–5,000 procurement episodes and roughly 20,000–60,000 candidate pairs if hardware permits. Counts are design targets, not measured results.

Generation order:

1. Create immutable hidden ground-truth episodes, items, supplier identities, policies, order revisions, receipts, invoices, credits, and cash events.
2. Generate clean source-system representations of those episodes.
3. Apply independent corruption/fragmentation operators to source representations.
4. Keep truth IDs and corruption labels in evaluation-only files.
5. Feed only observed source representations into the app.
6. Write a manifest listing seed, generator version, assumptions, evidence source, and counts by corruption.

Generate by ledger constraints, not by asking an LLM for a CSV of plausible invoices.

### 5.6 Realistic variation and controls

Positive variation:

- Vendor alias versus legal name.
- Punctuation/case/spacing in PO/invoice references.
- OCR confusions in a controlled document benchmark.
- Item synonym, abbreviation, equivalent supplier SKU with approved crosswalk.
- Known box-to-each conversion, with item-specific packaging evidence.
- Split receipt, split invoice, consolidated payment, partial payment.
- Invoice before receipt allowed by policy.
- Authorized credit note, return, cancelled order line, PO revision.
- Settled, pending, failed, and reversed transfers.

Hard negatives:

- Same supplier and amount but different PO, delivery, serial/batch, or billing period.
- Pump/motor descriptions differing in power, voltage, size, model, or capacity.
- Same invoice number from different suppliers.
- Same payment amount in different currencies.
- Repeated legitimate monthly invoices.
- Pro forma followed by commercial invoice.
- Corrected invoice that explicitly supersedes an earlier document.
- Re-uploaded copy of a source file, not a second business charge.
- A credit note whose sign would be incorrectly interpreted by a naive importer.

### 5.7 Data truth labels

Store labels outside production input:

- `episode_truth_id`, `entity_truth_id`, `item_truth_id`.
- `same_obligation`, `same_item`, `same_invoice_representation`.
- `duplicate_charge`, `legitimate_repeat`, `allocation_truth`.
- `discrepancy_types`, `expected_actions`, `expected_balances`.
- `injection_operator`, `injection_family`, `parent_episode_id`.

Inference loaders must reject truth columns. Candidate features must never include source filename tags such as `duplicate_01` or `correct_case`.

### 5.8 Split and provenance requirements

- Split base episodes before rendering/corrupting them.
- All variants, documents, lines, and duplicate copies of one episode stay in the same split.
- Use train/validation/calibration/test partitions; do not use test labels for threshold selection.
- Include supplier-disjoint, item-family-disjoint, time-forward, and unseen corruption-family holdouts.
- Evaluate observed-record linkage before any manually supplied canonical alias tables generated from test truth.
- Maintain separate source badges: `PUBLIC_REAL`, `PUBLIC_SYNTHETIC`, `DERIVED_FROM_PUBLIC`, `CONSTRUCTED`, `USER_PROVIDED`.
- “Derived from public” only applies when an actual downloaded source trace/distribution was used, with the mapping recorded.
- If no source download exists yet, call the simulator research-informed, not BPI-derived.

## 6. Golden demo scenarios and exact expected results

### 6.1 Flagship: partial fulfillment, duplicate billing, split settlement

Fixture: `DEMO-PO-1001`, fictional supplier `Meridian Pump Supply`, transaction currency INR.

Assumptions: each pump costs INR 1,000.00 net; the demo tax is 18% and is explicitly a fixture assumption, not a tax-compliance claim. Three-way matching; received units are accepted in this case. Order due date is later than the first replay point.

| Record | Observed fact |
|---|---|
| PO-1001 / line 1 | 100 pumps, net 100,000.00, tax 18,000.00, full-order gross 118,000.00 |
| GRN-1001-A | 40 pumps physically received |
| GRN-1001-B | 20 additional pumps physically received |
| INV-M-041 | 60 pumps, net 60,000.00, tax 10,800.00, gross 70,800.00 |
| AP-ROW-992 | A second AP posting with invoice ref `INV M 041`, same supplier, same obligation content |
| BANK-T01 | Settled INR 30,000.00; verified remittance identifies INV-M-041 |
| BANK-T02 | Settled INR 20,800.00; verified remittance identifies INV-M-041 |
| BANK-T03 | Settled INR 15,000.00, same supplier, no invoice reference |
| INV-M-042 | A separate, receipt-supported INR 15,000.00 invoice from the same supplier; not part of PO-1001 |

Source names differ: `Centrifugal pump CP-100 1kW` / `CP100 pump, 1 kW`; canonical linking also checks model and power.

Expected initial investigation:

- Ordered = 100; received = 60; not yet received = 40.
- Partial fulfillment is expected before its due date; not automatically a delivery failure.
- Both AP source rows remain visible; the second row is a suspected duplicate charge/representation pending confirmation.
- Verified cash applied to INV-M-041 = 30,000 + 20,800 = 50,800.
- Supported original invoice gross = 70,800.
- Supported original outstanding = 20,000, before allocating BANK-T03.
- Raw recorded invoice gross for the two AP postings = 141,600.
- Raw recorded open AP for those postings, assuming T01/T02 are posted against the original only = 90,800.
- The disputed difference is 70,800, not confirmed savings or already-paid duplicate loss.
- BANK-T03 remains unallocated because it could partly settle INV-M-041 or fully settle INV-M-042.
- Under the explicitly shown competing scenarios, PO-1001 outstanding lies between 5,000 and 20,000.
- Report the range as a feasible-scenario range, not a probability interval or a guaranteed legal liability range.
- Do not subtract T03 from both invoices or allocate it merely because 15,000 matches INV-M-042 exactly.

After reviewer confirms that AP-ROW-992 represents the same invoice rather than a legitimate extra charge:

- Canonical obligation gross for PO-1001 = 70,800.
- Confirmed settled allocation to original = 50,800.
- Canonical outstanding = 20,000; disputed duplicate posting = 70,800 excluded by recorded reviewer decision.
- Hold the duplicate posting / draft request for correction; do not delete the source row.

After separately verified remittance identifies T03 as payment for INV-M-042:

- INV-M-042 outstanding = 0.
- PO-1001 outstanding remains 20,000; ambiguity resolves.
- No cash movement occurred inside Satya. Only evidence-based allocation changed.

After an additional buyer GRN records the remaining 40 pumps:

- Received = 100; open delivery quantity = 0.
- Existing invoice outstanding stays 20,000.
- No new invoice means the additional 40 pumps may be received-but-unbilled; do not invent a 47,200 invoice.
- Full-order 118,000 must never be treated as the current payable balance just because all goods arrived.

### 6.3 Demo replay states

Use fixed event dates and a fixed `demoNow`; do not let tomorrow change the demo unexpectedly.

1. `ORDER_ONLY`: order visible; no overdue condition before deadline.
2. `PARTIAL_RECEIPT`: first receipt visible; fulfillment partial and legitimate.
3. `BILLED`: original invoice links to receipts/order; verify supported amount.
4. `DUPLICATE_POSTING`: discrepancy and duplicate alternatives appear.
5. `SPLIT_PAYMENT`: T01/T02 reduce balance; T03 remains uncertain.
6. `REVIEWED`: duplicate and remittance evidence resolve through separate decisions.
7. `LATE_DELIVERY`: final GRN resolves fulfillment, not automatically invoice/payment balance.

Each stage must be a real ingestion/reconciliation transition or a read-only as-of replay. UI animations are presentation only.

## 8. Canonical data contracts and database design

### 8.1 Common envelope

Every persisted business record includes:

```text
id: application UUID/string; stable across graph/UI/API
companyId: authorized tenant identifier
sourceSystem: erp | warehouse | supplier_portal | bank | file_import | bpi2019
sourceRecordId: identifier from source, not globally unique by itself
sourceRevision: monotonically versioned observation where supported
sourceDocumentId: raw source file/document reference
recordType: PO | INVOICE | GRN | ACCEPTANCE | PAYMENT | CREDIT_NOTE | RETURN | PROFORMA
occurredAt: source business event timestamp/date semantics
observedAt: when Satya ingested/learned the record
effectiveFrom/effectiveTo: business revision validity, where applicable
schemaVersion: e.g. business-record-v1
provenanceClass: PUBLIC_REAL | PUBLIC_SYNTHETIC | DERIVED_FROM_PUBLIC | CONSTRUCTED | USER_PROVIDED
fieldEvidence: field path -> evidence locator(s)
qualityIssues: typed list, not a global arbitrary quality score
supersedesRecordId / reversesRecordId: explicit if evidenced
```

A record's source reference is not necessarily a true unique business key. Preserve repeated AP postings; do not create a global unique index on vendor/invoice number that hides duplicate charges.

### 8.2 Decimal and money representation

API money:

```json
{"amount":"70800.00","currency":"INR"}
```

- Money, quantity, unit price, tax rate, exchange rate, and unit conversion ratios travel as decimal strings.
- Mongo stores exact money/quantity values using Decimal128; API serializers return plain strings, not BSON internals.
- Python uses `decimal.Decimal`, with explicitly configured rounding/precision for the policy.
- Currency display scale is a verified registry/configuration, not universally 2 decimals.
- Unit price may require more precision than settlement amount.
- Optimization may use currency minor units or declared quantity scale after bounds checks; never silently round away residuals.
- Do not use JavaScript `Number`, Python binary floats, or embedding scores for balances.
- UI can convert a value only for a bounded chart coordinate, never for canonical sums.
- Keep currencies separate in dashboards. A mixed-currency portfolio cannot be summed without an explicit dated FX policy and source.
- Decimal128 is supported by Mongo monetary-data documentation [R11]; it does not automatically make application-side math correct.

### 8.4 Type-specific fields

**Purchase order**

- vendorRef, buyer/legalEntity, status, orderNumber, revision, approvedAt.
- currency, deliverySchedules, warehouse/location, paymentTerms, contractRef.
- lines: sourceLineId, itemReference, rawDescription, specificationAttributes, quantity, uom, unitPrice, allowances, charges, taxComponents.
- matchingLevel by line/schedule, advance policy, cancellation/revision references.

**Invoice**

- invoiceNumberRaw/normalized, vendorRef, status, issueDate, dueDate, currency.
- orderReferences and receiptReferences preserve raw and parsed values.
- invoice kind: commercial/debit/correction; PI is a separate record type.
- lines: quantity, uom, item reference, description/specs, net price/net amount, tax/charge evidence.
- header totals: net, tax, charge, allowance, gross, rounding, declaredPrepaid, declaredPayable.
- declaredPrepaid is a supplier claim until matched to independently verified settlement/advance evidence.
- supersession/precedingInvoiceRef where present; supplier fiscal reference if supplied, but no invented tax identifier.

**GRN / acceptance**

- buyer receipt ID, PO line refs, physical quantity, UOM, warehouse, receiver/date.
- accepted/rejected quantities or separate acceptance records; unknown acceptance remains unknown.
- supplier shipment evidence must carry a distinct type/subtype and cannot count as buyer acceptance.

**Payment**

- bank transaction ID, payer/payee identity, status, settlement/value date.
- sourceCurrency/sourceAmount; settlement currency and amount if genuinely provided.
- raw references, remittance detail, fee evidence, FX evidence, reversal reference.
- allocation candidates are derived records, not overwritten raw bank facts.

**Credit / return / PI**

- Credit: positive magnitude with explicit `effect=reduce_obligation`; referenced invoice/line, tax components, reason, status.
- Return: quantity leaving buyer, receipt/line reference, acceptance status; not an automatic monetary credit.
- PI: preliminary request, amount/terms/ref; does not increase posted AP by default.

### 8.7 Essential indexes

- companyId on every tenant-owned collection; compound indexes begin with companyId for scoped listing.
- Business record identity: unique `(companyId, sourceSystem, sourceRecordId, sourceRevision)` only when source identity is trustworthy; otherwise use assigned ingestion row identity.
- Source bytes: dedupe import by `(companyId, checksum, adapterVersion, mappingVersion)`; preserve additional submission receipts if business/audit requires them.
- Lookup by `(companyId, recordType, vendorRef, occurredAt)` and normalized references.
- Job uniqueness on `(companyId, inputManifestHash, engineVersion)`.
- Case listing by company/status/updatedAt.
- Snapshot unique application ID plus company/case/time index.
- Decisions indexed by company/case/snapshot; source-specific invalidation references.
- Projection status unique `(companyId, snapshotId)`.

### 8.8 Consistency without assuming Mongo transactions exist

Current Compose MongoDB is a standalone instance. Multi-document transactions/change streams cannot simply be assumed available.

Core-compatible publication protocol:

1. Reserve/lease a persisted job with compare-and-swap.
2. Read immutable input revisions and the current case revision.
3. Compute a complete result in Python.
4. Store one immutable snapshot/result manifest; for large components, write referenced chunks first.
5. Atomically mark the job/result publication committed only after all referenced chunks exist.
6. Update case `latestSnapshotId` with expected case revision; if inputs changed, retain the old snapshot but do not publish it as latest.
7. Projection worker reads committed snapshots and idempotently upserts Neo4j.
8. UI displays projection lag/version, not a false distributed-transaction guarantee.
9. Startup reconciliation repairs partially published jobs/orphan chunks; source data never depends on Neo4j availability.

A future replica-set upgrade can support a transactional outbox, but requires deliberate deployment work and testing; do not claim the standalone design is an ACID transaction across databases.

## 9. Ingestion and source reconstruction

### 9.1 Structured-first path

MUST support CSV and JSON exports for purchase orders, invoices, receipts, payments, and credits. These qualify as multi-source records without requiring OCR first.

SHOULD support XLSX after a safe parser is selected. Existing `xlsx` has known security concerns recorded in the template's earlier history; do not build a public, unrestricted spreadsheet-upload service around it without a dependency/security review.

UI flow:

1. Select source system and record type.
2. Upload file or choose a seeded connector/export.
3. Show column/field mapping with sample rows.
4. Show validation errors and ambiguities before commit.
5. Commit a batch; track persistent progress.
6. Inspect row-level successes, quarantined rows, and duplicate import detections.
7. Trigger reconciliation only for affected supplier/order components.

### 9.2 Upload safety

- Authenticate and authorize source upload before accepting content.
- Limit bytes, rows, columns, page count, nested JSON depth, and processing time.
- Check extension AND actual content type; never trust a supplied filename.
- Store under server-generated identifiers, not user paths.
- Never serve raw uploads from an unauthenticated static directory.
- Prevent CSV spreadsheet formula injection on exported reports.
- Reject XML external entities, macro-enabled spreadsheets unless explicitly supported, and archive bombs.
- No fetching arbitrary user-supplied URLs; connector URLs are allowlisted server configuration.
- No confidential documents sent to an external LLM by default; an explicit company setting and UI notice are required.
- Preserve checksum and byte-exact original, subject to retention policy.
- A document hash helps integrity/dedupe; it does not prove issuer authenticity.

### 9.3 Normalization rules

- Unicode normalization, trim/collapse whitespace, preserve the raw string.
- Parse decimal separators only with a chosen locale/source mapping; `1,000` cannot be guessed as either one or one thousand.
- Date ambiguity such as `03/04/2026` requires a source locale or review; store date precision and timezone semantics.
- IDs: retain leading zeros; normalize punctuation for matching, not original display.
- Names: approved vendor aliases, normalized legal suffixes as features; legal suffix stripping is not proof of identity.
- Product attributes: preserve model numbers, voltage, power, dimensions, and packaging. Never erase discriminatory numbers as stop words.
- Unit conversion uses approved item-specific catalog mappings and effective dates.
- Tax-inclusive/exclusive semantics must be explicit. Missing tax components stay unknown.
- Unknown critical monetary/currency/quantity fields quarantine that record from automatic balances.
- Structured parser validates syntax/schema but does not manufacture an extraction-confidence percentage.

### 9.4 PDF and scanned-document path: optional until core is stable

Recommended adapter: Docling local pipeline, because its primary repository supports local execution, PDF/table/OCR processing, and structured document exports [R12]. DeepWiki also identified provenance-preserving JSON exports; verify APIs against the pinned installed version.

Important limitation: **generic table extraction is not invoice semantic extraction.** Build a separate invoice-field mapping/validation layer. Correct table geometry does not guarantee the correct invoice total was identified.

Adapter output must include:

- Raw parser output, parser/model revision, warnings, per-page provenance.
- Page/token/table-cell locations used for each canonical field.
- Missing or conflicting candidates rather than one silently chosen total.
- Header-total/line-total arithmetic check.
- Review gate for ambiguous currency, invoice ID, quantity, tax, or total.
- Editable extraction preview; original file remains unchanged.

Fallback order:

1. Structured CSV/JSON import and seeded fixtures.
2. Digital PDF text/tables with provenance.
3. OCR only when required by a scanned source.
4. External vision LLM only by explicit configuration, strict schema, evidence spans, and review.

Never treat a model's self-reported confidence as calibrated extraction probability. Benchmark extraction independently on withheld layouts/fields; FATURA is relevant here, not for end-to-end settlement labels.

### 9.5 Connector completeness

Record a connector/export coverage manifest:

```text
sourceSystem, periodStart, periodEnd, importedAt
coverage: complete | partial | unknown
lastSuccessfulSyncAt, expectedCadence, timezone
recordCount, rejectedCount, checksum, sourceScope
```

A “missing receipt” finding must say whether warehouse coverage supports that claim. If the connector is stale, label `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE_COVERAGE`; do not blame the supplier.

### 9.6 Idempotency levels

- Same import file/mapping repeated: reuse batch result or record submission as a no-op.
- Same source row ID/revision: reuse observation unless bytes changed; changed bytes require revision/conflict handling.
- Same invoice represented in ERP and supplier export: two source observations can represent one obligation.
- Separate AP posting of the same bill: preserve both postings and flag duplication; do not dedupe away evidence of an accounting error.
- Different invoice numbers with similar content: suspected duplicates, not automatic merge.

## 10. Matching architecture and model specification

### 10.1 Separate identity from correctness

The linkage question is: **“Do these records refer to the same order/item/obligation?”**

The discrepancy question is: **“Do their quantities, terms, and amounts agree under policy?”**

A genuinely linked invoice can have a wrong amount or currency. Do not hard-reject such candidates just because amounts disagree; that would hide the discrepancy we need to detect.

No single scorer solves all relation types. Distinguish:

- vendor record -> vendor master.
- invoice line -> PO line.
- receipt line -> PO line.
- invoice representation -> invoice representation.
- payment/remittance -> invoice obligation.
- credit -> invoice/line.

Use relation-specific validation, features, thresholds, and calibration metadata.

### 10.2 Candidate generation

Avoid all-pairs comparisons. Generate the union of bounded candidate blocks:

1. Company scope: mandatory hard boundary.
2. Strong explicit reference: PO/invoice/receipt reference normalized but raw preserved.
3. Reviewed supplier identity + broad date window.
4. Item model/SKU/specification overlap.
5. Text retrieval: char/token TF-IDF and optional semantic top-k within authorized supplier/order scope.
6. Amount/quantity neighborhood as additional retrieval, not sole proof.
7. Unreferenced records from the same company/vendor within configured open-order/payment horizon.

Default development caps, configurable and observable:

- Top 30 line candidates per input line.
- Top 20 invoice candidates per ambiguous payment.
- Broad date window default 120 days for equipment flows; this is a demo setting, not a universal rule.
- Strong references outside the window still get checked and receive a timing warning.
- If a block truncates candidates, surface `CANDIDATE_LIMIT_REACHED`; no false confidence from unseen alternatives.

Evaluate candidate recall before evaluating classifier accuracy. A perfect model cannot recover an excluded correct candidate.

### 10.3 Deterministic baseline

Version `rules-link-v1`:

- Exact verified source references plus supplier/company consistency.
- Reviewed supplier aliases and SKU/UOM crosswalks.
- Transparent feature-weighted heuristic for suggestions.
- One-to-one mapping only in components known to be one-to-one.
- Missing references and ties remain review items.
- Heuristic scores are called scores, not probabilities.

This baseline delivers the first demo and remains the fallback if learned artifacts fail to load.

### 10.4 Feature schema for trained pair model

Use the existing scikit-learn/XGBoost dependencies before introducing a new large training framework.

Common observed features:

```text
reference_exact, reference_edit_similarity, reference_missing
vendor_verified_same, vendor_alias_similarity, vendor_missing
sku_exact, model_token_agreement, specification_conflict_count
char_ngram_cosine, token_jaccard, lexical_token_similarity
semantic_cosine, semantic_available
unit_compatible, conversion_verified, quantity_ratio, quantity_missing
net_price_ratio, absolute_amount_delta, relative_amount_delta
currency_equal, currency_missing
signed_date_gap_days, date_missing, source_date_quality
explicit_receipt_reference, explicit_remittance_reference
billing_period_overlap, warehouse_consistent
source_field_quality_count, critical_field_conflict_count
```

- Zero amount/quantity denominators require defined flags, not division errors.
- Missing is different from disagreement.
- Feature vectors derive from observations known at the snapshot's observation cutoff.
- A label/case type, future decision, ground-truth ID, or later receipt is prohibited.
- Raw invoice numbers/vendor IDs should not become memorization features in a supplier-disjoint evaluation.
- Explain matching features and rule evidence separately; SHAP is not a substitute for business evidence.

### 10.5 Trained models

**Model A / required trained ML: `pair-link-xgb-v1`**

- Binary classifier per core relation or a shared model with explicitly validated relation context.
- Start with logistic regression as interpretable baseline.
- Compare XGBoost using shallow trees and regularization; suggested search space: depth 3–6, 100–400 trees, learning rate 0.03–0.1, subsample/column sample 0.7–1.0.
- Search ranges are proposals, not a promise that those hyperparameters are optimal.
- Use episode-grouped validation. Select by precision/coverage/cost constraints, not maximum raw accuracy.
- Train with realistic hard negatives from candidate blocks, not only random unrelated invoices.
- No SMOTE by default: interpolated pair features can violate reference/specification constraints.

**Model B / semantic representation: `item-embed-minilm-v1`**

- Initial model candidate: `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, verified 384-dimensional English encoder with Apache-2.0 model-card license [R13].
- Pin model revision and artifact hashes; pre-cache for offline demo.
- It is a pretrained representation, not a model trained on the team's data unless fine-tuned.
- Represent description + model/SKU + unit/specification labels with a documented serialization.
- Cosine similarity is retrieval evidence, never a calibrated same-item probability.
- Hybrid lexical+semantic retrieval; hard specification conflicts remain visible.
- Fine-tuning is STRETCH using episode-disjoint positive pairs and carefully constructed hard negatives.

**Model C / duplicate scorer: `invoice-duplicate-xgb-v1` SHOULD**

- Train a relation-specific model using invoice ref similarity, line fingerprints, dates, periods, supplier and PO evidence.
- Separate output for same representation vs suspected duplicate charge.
- Similarity cannot decide which posting is canonical or whether a credit/supersession makes it legitimate.

**Model D / arrival likelihood: STRETCH**

- If real timing data is usable, survival/time-to-event modeling or empirical conditional delay quantiles can support “likely delayed evidence” context.
- Account for incomplete traces/right censoring; do not label absent future events as zero delay.
- Output delay estimates with dataset/cohort scope, never override a control rule or manufacture a receipt.

**Models not required:** GNN, reinforcement learning, federated learning, custom OCR from scratch, giant fine-tuned LLM. Only add one if an ablation demonstrates a useful gain on independently held-out data.

### 10.6 More advanced alternatives

- Fellegi–Sunter probabilistic record linkage provides interpretable match evidence via agreement likelihood ratios [R14]. Consider it for vendor/reference identity linkage or as a baseline.
- Its conditional-independence assumptions can be violated by correlated reference/amount/description features. Do not multiply dependent evidence blindly or present the resulting probability as guaranteed calibrated.
- Retrieve-and-rerank with a cross-encoder can improve difficult description comparison [R15], but generic search relevance is not business record identity. Fine-tune/evaluate on actual pair labels before using its score as match probability.
- Splink/cross-encoder are optional additional dependencies; do not replace a working lightweight baseline without measured benefit.

### 10.7 Calibration and abstention

Fit calibration on a held-out calibration set after model selection, not training/test data.

- Start with sigmoid calibration when labeled support is limited; compare isotonic only with enough held-out samples.
- Show reliability curve, bin sample sizes, Brier score, log loss, and optional ECE.
- ECE depends on binning; document bins and show the curve, not one magic reliability number.
- Existing scikit-learn is pinned to 1.5.2. Use APIs supported by that installed version; current docs may describe newer wrappers [R16].
- Calibration on constructed data does not prove reliability on real equipment business records.
- Pair probabilities are conditional on the candidate-generation population. Candidate prevalence and deployment shift matter.

Provisional development bands (not measured precision):

```text
p >= 0.98 + no critical contradiction + margin >= 0.10 -> strong proposal
0.70 <= p < 0.98 or small alternative margin -> needs reviewer/evidence
p < 0.70 -> unresolved/unlinked, retain search evidence
```

These values MUST be tuned on validation/calibration evidence. Until then label them `provisional`.

Auto-linking is permitted only when policy/evaluation readiness allows it; auto-linking does not authorize payment or merge suspicious obligations.

If there is no trained artifact or no valid calibration:

- Set `matchProbability=null`.
- Display rule/heuristic evidence and qualitative confidence.
- Mark model readiness honestly.
- Continue deterministic reconciliation and the target demo.

### 10.8 Confidence representation

Do not combine everything into a deceptive single percentage.

```text
extractionQuality: validated | review_required | unreliable | unknown
linkConfidence: probability or heuristic score + kind + model + evaluation scope
ruleCertainty: proven_from_inputs | conditional | insufficient_evidence
sourceCoverage: complete | partial | stale | unknown
decisionStatus: suggested | reviewer_confirmed | reviewer_rejected
```

A mathematically certain variance can rest on an uncertain link. Display both.

Reviewer confirmation is authority/decision evidence, not an automatically perfect ML label.

## 11. Global constraints, many-to-many relationships, and allocation

### 11.1 Two distinct maps

Maintain:

1. **Commercial attribution map:** which PO/obligation an invoice or receipt refers to.
2. **Support/settlement allocation map:** how much receipt quantity or settled cash supports each invoice.

Do not cap invoice identity linkage at received quantity. An overbilling invoice must still link to the right PO and reveal unsupported billed units.

### 11.2 One-to-one baseline

For truly one-to-one line components, use SciPy `linear_sum_assignment` with explicit unmatched dummy nodes [R17].

- The solver's actual implementation need not be called “Hungarian”; SciPy documents modified Jonker–Volgenant.
- A rectangular matrix alone does not safely prevent undesirable forced matches.
- Add unmatched options with documented costs and prohibit invalid arcs.
- Keep ties/near-ties for review.

This is not sufficient for split shipments or split/consolidated payments.

### 11.3 Quantity support allocation

Within a company/item/order component:

- Receipt buckets have available eligible quantity after reversals/returns/inspection rules.
- Invoice buckets have billed quantities linked to that order line.
- Edges exist only for compatible item/UOM/policy context.
- Allocate eligible quantity without reusing the same receipt units across obligations.
- Prior allocations and reviewer locks are constraints.
- Unsupported billed remainder is a discrepancy output, not an infeasible solver failure.
- Unbilled receipt quantity remains visible.
- If the business allows pooled receipt support across legitimate invoices, choose a documented deterministic allocation order; do not confuse it with identified shipment pairing.

### 11.4 Payment allocation

Use verified remittance first, then propose allocations for unresolved bank transfers.

- Only settled, non-reversed payment cash contributes to settlement.
- Supplier/company and currency compatibility are mandatory for settled allocation; evidenced cross-currency handling is a separate transformation.
- One payment may allocate to several invoices; one invoice may receive several payments.
- A transfer may remain partly or fully unapplied.
- An advance remains advance/unapplied until an evidenced invoice application exists.
- Strong amount coincidence cannot override missing reference ambiguity.
- Distinguish cash amount from invoice reduction due to discounts/withholding/credits.

### 11.5 Core constraint formulation

Let `x[p,i]` be non-negative cash allocation from settled payment p to invoice i, represented in validated currency minor units.

```text
sum_i x[p,i] <= allocatable_settled_cash[p]
sum_p x[p,i] <= currently_open_obligation[i] unless evidenced overpayment handling
x[p,i] = 0 for prohibited supplier/company/currency edges
reviewer_locked allocation = recorded amount
unapplied[p] = allocatable_settled_cash[p] - sum_i x[p,i]
```

Allocation variables alone permit arbitrary splits. Add relation evidence, candidate bounds, fragment penalties, and reviewer locks. Always keep an unapplied option so the objective cannot manufacture a settlement.

Suggested objective:

```text
minimize evidence-mismatch cost
       + unsupported-link cost
       + unnecessary-fragmentation cost
       + residual cost chosen conservatively
```

Residual penalty must not be so large that it forces weak links merely to make totals match. Solver objective values are optimization scores, not probabilities.

### 11.6 Solver selection

- Use NetworkX flow where a standard capacitated network suffices (already available).
- OR-Tools min-cost flow / CP-SAT is a planned optional dependency for integer capacities, binary activation, exclusivity, and alternate scenarios [R18].
- CP-SAT suits extra logical constraints; min-cost flow is faster for simpler flow structures.
- For quantity conversion, scale with declared precision and reject unsafe/unrepresentable scales.
- Version the cost function, time limit, random seed, worker count, and solver options.
- On timeout, distinguish a feasible solution from proven optimum; no result means review, not fabricated allocation.
- Deterministic verified remittance allocation remains available if optional solver tooling fails.

### 11.7 Duplicate constraints

- Candidate duplicate groups do not automatically collapse obligations.
- Confirmed representations of the same invoice share one canonical obligation with many source observations.
- Suspected separate duplicate charges remain disputed alternatives until review/evidence confirms treatment.
- Recurring invoices with different periods/receipts may coexist.
- Use explicit pair decisions and cluster coherence; A≈B and B≈C does not imply A≈C.
- Avoid transitive union-find across weak similarity edges that merges an entire month's supplier billing.
- At most one obligation can consume a confirmed identical invoice representation unless a documented correction changes it.

### 11.8 Alternative solutions

To expose an ambiguous transfer/link:

1. Compute best feasible proposal.
2. Freeze known facts and reviewer locks.
3. Prohibit/change the ambiguous candidate and resolve alternatives.
4. Compare cost, evidence differences, and balances.
5. If multiple explanations remain materially plausible, keep them unresolved.

A cost gap is not automatically a calibrated probability margin. Report its kind/version and reason.

### 11.9 Scenario balance bounds

Compute minimum and maximum outstanding only across explicitly defined feasible alternative allocations/obligations.

- Name assumptions: duplicate treatment, valid invoice identities, allowed FX, accepted receipts, possible payment links.
- Show which scenario attains each bound.
- Bounds from exact bounded optimization can be labeled certified under those assumptions.
- Enumerated partial alternatives provide only observed scenario range, not certified global bounds.
- On solver limit/incomplete hypotheses, display unknown or sampled scenario range with warning.
- No credible range when missing required currency/amount data is unbounded.
- This is not a statistical confidence interval.

## 12. Deterministic financial and quantity reconciliation

### 12.1 Mathematical authority

All canonical calculations run in the Python engine and produce machine-readable derivation objects. The same result powers dashboard, reports, and chatbot.

Node must not independently recompute a slightly different balance for an API response. Validate returned schema/invariants and persist the artifact.

### 12.2 Quantity definitions

For a PO line and snapshot:

```text
effective_ordered = approved_quantity - evidenced_cancelled_quantity
received_net = valid_buyer_receipts - receipt_reversals - applicable physical returns
accepted_net = evidenced_acceptances - acceptance_reversals - applicable accepted returns
eligible_receipt_qty = received_net for three-way, accepted_net for four-way
open_delivery_qty = max(effective_ordered - received_net, 0)
unsupported_billed_qty = max(valid_billed_qty - allocated_eligible_support_qty, 0)
received_unbilled_qty = max(eligible_receipt_qty - allocated_eligible_support_qty, 0)
```

- These sums require compatible units and revision semantics.
- Accepted quantity cannot exceed received quantity without an input-quality contradiction.
- Returns must not be subtracted once in receipts and again in an already-net accepted figure.
- Reversal/amendment identity determines whether a value replaces or offsets the original.
- Overdelivery is distinct from open quantity; do not hide it by clamping every total.
- In two-way service flows, eligibility follows contract/milestone policy, not fictional receipts.

### 12.3 Invoice arithmetic

For the chosen verified convention:

```text
line_net = quantity * unit_price - line_allowances + line_charges
invoice_net = sum(line_net) - header_allowances + header_charges
invoice_gross = invoice_net + tax_total
payable_basis = invoice_gross + payable_rounding
supplier_declared_payable = payable_basis - supplier_declared_prepaid
```

Different sources may provide net prices/amounts directly; do not add charges twice when source totals already include them.

Peppol's documented total/paid/rounding relationships inform validation [R19]. This does not make Satya a Peppol-compliant network sender or a tax engine.

Compare extracted header totals to reconstructed totals. Preserve both if they disagree.

### 12.4 Obligation and settlement ledgers

Show at least three distinct views:

1. **Recorded AP:** what supplied ERP/AP rows say is posted/open.
2. **Reconstructed obligation:** verified unique invoice obligations, accepted credits/corrections, under explicit duplicate treatment.
3. **Receipt-supported eligible amount:** component eligible for approval under matching policy; not necessarily the entire legal/booked liability.

For each canonical invoice/currency:

```text
net_obligation = canonical_payable_basis - valid_applied_credit_amount
verified_settlement = settled_cash_allocations + verified_applied_advances
                    + authorized_non_cash_settlement_adjustments
outstanding = net_obligation - verified_settlement
```

- Do not subtract supplier-declared prepaid AND the same verified advance allocation twice.
- Credits reduce obligation; bank refunds/reversals affect cash/settlement. Link them without double subtraction.
- Negative outstanding signals potential overpayment/credit; preserve it with reason, rather than silently clamping to zero.
- Payment fee is a separate fact; whether it reduces amount applied depends on evidence/policy.
- Authorized discount/withholding requires a separate typed adjustment and supporting evidence. Legitimate partial payment/discount examples are documented by Dynamics [R21], but possible explanations are not evidence that a particular adjustment applies.
- Payment due date/terms determine whether supported outstanding is payable now, future-due, or overdue.
- “Approve” can mean eligible for a payment proposal, not “pay today regardless of terms.”

### 12.5 Price comparison

- Compare net unit prices after verified UOM conversion and allowances.
- Use the approved PO revision effective at the relevant business time.
- Quantity difference can be a split invoice, not a wrong price.
- Separate base price, freight, tax, discount, and FX effects.
- If tax/fee data is incomplete, report incomplete comparison rather than unexplained total mismatch as fraud.

### 12.6 Currency handling

- PO/invoice currency conflict can coexist with identity linkage and must be flagged.
- Cash allocation cannot cross currencies without explicit conversion evidence/policy.
- Record rate source, rate date, pair direction, rounding, and who authorized the conversion.
- Distinguish transaction amount, accounting-currency display, and actual bank-settled amount.
- Offline demo FX is a seeded fixture rate labeled `demo_assumption`, not a live market rate.
- Unknown rate means no converted aggregate and no automatic mismatch explanation.
- Do not promise full realized/unrealized FX accounting in the core.

### 12.7 Critical invariants

1. Input/file re-import cannot change liability or cash.
2. Sum of payment allocations plus unapplied amount equals eligible settled cash after explicit fees/reversals.
3. Sum of receipt support allocations cannot exceed eligible receipt quantity.
4. Each confirmed canonical invoice is counted once.
5. Suspected duplicates cannot disappear from recorded AP without a decision/evidence trace.
6. Credits/advances/discounts are applied once, with typed effects.
7. Different currencies are not summed without an evidenced conversion.
8. Source row revisions and event reversals cannot be counted as new independent events by default.
9. An unresolved linkage cannot be presented as confirmed settlement.
10. A late-arriving record changes new snapshots, not the record of what was known earlier.
11. UI and chat use the same snapshot ID and amounts.
12. No accounting value originates from an LLM response.

```text
UNEXPLAINED_PAYMENT_SHORTFALL

RETURN_AWAITING_CREDIT
SOURCE_COVERAGE_GAP

```

Each finding is generated by a versioned deterministic rule or explicitly marked ML suspicion. Known rules do not need a model trained to rediscover arithmetic.

### 13.2 Finding contract

```text
findingId, caseId, snapshotId, type, affectedRecords/lines
observedFacts: evidence-backed values
expectedFacts: policy/order-backed values
calculation: formula ID, operands/evidence, output/currency
confidenceBreakdown, sourceCoverage
category: costly_error | expected_variance | timing_pending | ambiguous | data_quality
severity: info | review | high | critical
exposure: amount/range/null + kind + assumptions + currency
recommendedAction, ownerRole, reason, requiredEvidence, dueAt
status: open | acknowledged | resolved | invalidated
```

The solver not finding an allocation is not enough to classify fraud or costly error.

### 13.3 Timing and omission rules

- Open delivery before promised date: expected partial fulfillment.
- Invoice before receipt with valid invoice-before-GRN policy: waiting for evidence; payment eligibility may remain held.
- Receipt absence after delivery date with complete warehouse coverage: overdue/missing receipt evidence.
- Warehouse coverage unknown/stale: source gap first.
- Received-but-unbilled within expected billing window: timing pending.
- No commercial invoice beyond a configured window: missing invoice evidence, not invented supplier liability.
- Payment pending/failed/reversed: not settled cash.
- Source-specific grace windows are versioned demo policy, not universal business facts.

### 13.4 Four actions and prerequisites

| Action | Meaning in Satya | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Approve | Mark eligible amount/scope for authorized finance workflow | Strong/confirmed links, required evidence, no unresolved blocking findings, correct terms |
| Request Clarification | Draft a targeted request for missing/contradictory information | Explicit question and source gaps; actual sending not automatic |
| Escalate | Assign unresolved/high-exposure case to authorized reviewer | Materiality/policy trigger; not a fraud verdict |
| Place on Hold | Block further internal approval on the specific posting/amount | Typed blocking reason and release prerequisite |

Actions apply to a specific invoice/posting/line/amount, not automatically an entire supplier or PO.

If duplicate money was already paid, a hold cannot recover it. Recommend escalation/recovery request draft; record suspected paid exposure separately.

### 13.5 Recommendation policy

Suggested demo configuration:

- Exact imported amount inconsistency: hold that document pending extraction/source clarification.
- Suspected duplicate posting: hold duplicate candidate, preserve original eligibility under manager review.
- Overbilling quantity/acceptance: hold unsupported amount; approve eligible remainder only if policy permits partial approval.
- Ambiguous payment: request remittance; do not credit competing invoices simultaneously.
- Expected partial delivery before due date: informational; no unnecessary hold.
- Unknown currency/UOM: request clarification; block automatic settlement reconciliation.
- High exposure or conflicting reviewer evidence: escalate.

Materiality and approval limits must be company settings. Example thresholds are demo assumptions, not universal financial controls.

### 13.6 Prioritization without false savings

Use lexicographic priority rather than a misleading opaque financial risk score:

1. Confirmed integrity/input failure blocking money decisions.
2. Already-paid suspected duplicate/overpayment exposure.
3. High-value pending duplicate or unsupported billing.
4. Imminent due/discount deadline with actionable evidence gap.
5. Ordinary unresolved/expected timing variance.

Within a band sort by exposure, due date, then age. If ranking uses a weighted score, disclose weights and keep money unchanged.

Dashboard amounts:

- Pending disputed exposure.
- Already-paid suspected exposure.
- Receipt-supported payable pending review.
- Verified outstanding by currency.
- Unallocated settled cash.
- Resolved prevented/recovered amount only when an audited action supports it.

Do not sum overlapping duplicate/quantity/tax findings into one “total savings” number. Aggregate deduplicated obligation components, with overlap policy and evidence.

### 13.7 Resolution preview

Inputs: base snapshot + hypothetical change, such as new GRN, supplier credit, remittance allocation, or candidate-link decision.

Outputs:

- Before/after quantities, outstanding, eligible amount, findings, and graph diff.
- Assumptions and newly satisfied prerequisites.
- “Simulation only” watermark and no mutation of source/real case state.
- A separate authorized confirmation creates an actual reviewer decision/new source observation, not automatic promotion of a hypothetical document.

## 14. Neo4j evidence graph and temporal replay

### 14.1 Purpose

The graph answers relationship questions that are awkward to understand in isolated spreadsheets:

- Which order, receipt, invoice, payment, and credit are connected?
- Which evidence supports this match or finding?
- Is an edge a source reference, a machine suggestion, or reviewer-confirmed allocation?
- What changed when new evidence arrived?

The graph is an explanation/read model. Canonical money remains in the persisted reconciliation snapshot.

### 14.2 Node labels

```text
Company, Supplier, CatalogItem
SourceDocument, RecordRevision, LineRevision
PurchaseOrder, Invoice, GoodsReceipt, Acceptance, Payment, CreditNote
Case, ReconciliationSnapshot, Finding, Evidence, Decision
```

Use additional labels for type on the same record node where convenient; avoid duplicating the same logical document as unrelated nodes.

All tenant-owned nodes include `companyId`, stable application `id`, revision identity, and projection version. Do not use Neo4j `elementId` as a public business identity or durable external key.

### 14.3 Relationship types

| Edge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HAS_LINE | Header contains observed line |
| ISSUED_BY / ORDERED_FROM | Document supplier link |
| OBSERVED_IN | Record/field came from source document |
| SOURCE_REFERENCES | Raw source explicitly references another record |
| CANDIDATE_MATCH | Model/rule proposal, not an accepted accounting link |
| CONFIRMED_MATCH | Reviewer/rule-authorized identity linkage |
| SUPPORTS_QUANTITY | Receipt/acceptance quantity supports invoice portion |
| SETTLES | Verified or reviewer-accepted payment amount allocation |
| CANDIDATE_SETTLEMENT | Unresolved payment alternative |
| CREDITS | Credit reduces a specified obligation |
| REVERSES / SUPERSEDES | Evidenced lifecycle relation |
| SUSPECTED_DUPLICATE_OF | Duplicate suspicion, not automatic exclusion |
| REPRESENTATION_OF | Confirmed multiple source views of one invoice |
| SUPPORTS_FINDING | Evidence contributes to deterministic/conditional finding |
| GENERATED_IN | Link/finding belongs to snapshot |
| REVIEWED_BY_DECISION | Decision authority/reason link |

Edges carry relation ID, snapshotId, rule/modelVersion, evidenceIds, confidenceKind/value, status, and applicable quantity/money as decimal strings. Unresolved edge cannot be mislabeled `SETTLES`.

### 14.4 Projection behavior

- Worker projects only committed snapshots from Mongo.
- Use idempotent MERGE on stable scoped node/edge identities.
- Preserve source revisions and snapshot-specific derived edges; old snapshot edges never become current implicitly.
- Create supported uniqueness constraints after validating syntax against deployed Neo4j 5.26; current documentation may include newer features [R20].
- Constraints on nodes use `(companyId,id)` or a scoped synthetic key.
- Cap per-case projection size; paginate large supplier history rather than render every record.
- No graph-data-science plugin needed for the core. Remove that startup burden only as a deliberate deployment change.
- Graph unavailable: ledger/cases remain functional; show graph degraded/projection pending.
- Rebuild graph from snapshots without deleting Mongo data.
- Production privilege should be least-privilege; chatbot never receives database credentials.

### 14.5 Query constraints

- Every query is an allowlisted template with parameters.
- Every root lookup checks company membership and authorized case/snapshot.
- Every traversed node/edge is filtered to the same company and intended snapshot/revision scope.
- Bounded paths: at most 3 hops by default, nodes <= 200, edges <= 400, with explicit truncation.
- No free-text Cypher execution endpoint.
- User descriptions rendered as escaped labels/text, never query fragments.
- Graph lookup returns original record/evidence refs for frontend navigation.
- Supplier commonality alone is not proof that documents settle the same transaction.

### 14.6 UI graph grammar

- PO: blue; receipt/acceptance: green; invoice: purple; payment: teal; credit: cyan; finding: orange/red.
- Solid relation: observed or confirmed; dashed: candidate; faded: inactive/reversed; dotted: hypothetical.
- Label monetary/quantity edges, including unapplied remainder.
- Node click opens evidence/record drawer.
- Edge click opens candidate comparison and confidence breakdown.
- Path highlight answers a copilot question; it is an actual returned query path, not generated artwork.
- Use a layered PO -> receipt -> invoice -> payment layout for case explanation; retain pan/zoom for wider graph.
- Provide equivalent accessible table/list view; do not depend on color alone.

### 14.7 Two clocks: event time and knowledge time

Every replay request contains:

```text
occurredBefore: business events up to this cutoff
observedBefore: evidence/decisions Satya knew by this cutoff
```

A GRN for 5 October uploaded on 9 October:

- Is absent from “as known on 6 October.”
- Appears in a later reconstruction of events through 6 October, with late-arrival badge.
- Does not retroactively rewrite the audit record of a hold issued on 6 October.

Choose effective source revisions and reviewer decisions visible at the knowledge cutoff. Materialize a snapshot or compute read-only replay; never mutate current source state to animate a timeline.

- Server persists jobs; one bounded worker is adequate initially.
- Job lease/heartbeat prevents duplicate active publication; process restart can retry immutable input.
- Python may compute a bounded synchronous request off the async event loop; heavy jobs belong in controlled worker processes.
- No claim that in-memory FastAPI tasks survive restarts.
- Timeout retries are idempotent; a result received after a lease changed cannot overwrite a newer publication.
- Progress is truthful stage state, not invented percentages. Indeterminate stages are acceptable.

### 16.6 SSE / Socket.IO events

```text

reconciliation.stage, reconciliation.completed, reconciliation.failed

chat.tool.started, chat.tool.completed, chat.fact, chat.artifact, chat.delta, chat.completed
```

Payload always includes company-scoped IDs, request/job/snapshot version, timestamp, and resume cursor where supported.

- Authenticated fetch-stream SSE avoids query-string bearer tokens.
- Persist final results and critical stage events; reconnect reads state/cursor.
- Clean up connection listeners/heartbeats on disconnect.
- Socket handshake verifies JWT plus current membership.
- Room names assigned by server and checked against case ownership; no self-asserted `role`/`userId` joins.
- Do not assume Socket.IO client is installed; use existing fetch SSE first or add a verified dependency deliberately.

## 18. Training, evaluation, and scientific honesty

### 18.1 Evaluation layers

Evaluate independently:

1. Import/schema/normalization correctness.
2. Candidate-generation recall.
3. Pairwise relation classification.
4. Duplicate cluster correctness.
5. Quantity/cash allocation correctness.
6. End-to-end balances/findings/actions.
7. Citation/factuality and authorization of chatbot.
8. Reviewer usability and latency.

A high pair AUC does not establish correct invoice balances or safe approvals.

### 18.2 Required baselines and ablations

Compare on the same held-out episodes:

- Exact reference rules.
- Lexical/heuristic pair matching.
- Logistic regression.
- XGBoost on observed features.
- XGBoost + semantic features.
- Independent greedy link selection.
- Constrained allocation with the same pair scores.

Report failure examples and tradeoffs. If semantic embeddings/global optimization do not help, do not claim they do; retain only if they add demonstrable value or useful uncertainty handling.

### 18.3 Core metrics

| Layer | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Candidate retrieval | Recall@k, candidate count, truncated components |
| Pair linkage | Precision/recall/F1, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC as secondary, by relation |
| Duplicate detection | Pair metrics + coherent cluster precision/recall; legitimate-repeat false positives |
| Calibration | Reliability plot, bin counts, Brier/log loss, documented ECE |
| Selective linking | Precision vs coverage curve, abstention rate, false-link rate |
| Cash allocation | Exact allocation correctness, amount-weighted absolute error, unapplied cash correctness |
| Quantity support | Exact allocation correctness and invariant violations |
| End-to-end | Exact balances per currency/case, finding precision/recall by type, correct action scope |
| Chat | Citation validity, exact financial fact agreement, unsupported-claim rate, abstention quality |
| UX | Scripted reviewer task success/time with stated participant count |
| Ops | p50/p95 latency, import throughput, peak memory, timeout/degraded-path behavior |

Metrics separated by constructed/public-process/public-document origins; never blend them into one headline accuracy.

### 18.4 Acceptance targets versus results

Proposed targets for development, not achieved claims:

- 100% core ledger invariant pass on fixture/property tests.
- Exact expected balances/actions on all golden scenarios.
- Candidate recall >= 98% on held-out constructed pairs, with documented k and scope.
- >= 98% precision for high-confidence auto-link candidates on constructed holdout, accompanied by sample size and confidence interval.
- Show coverage at that precision; a model that abstains on everything is not useful.
- Zero cross-company access in automated tests.
- 100% golden chat financial cards reproduce underlying snapshot facts.
- Citation IDs resolve for every displayed evidence-linked monetary claim.

If a target is not achieved, keep auto-linking disabled or narrow its eligible scope; report actual results.

### 18.5 Leakage prevention

- Base episodes grouped across every corruption/rendered variation.
- Preserve chronological cutoffs; no later receipts, credits, resolutions, or supplier decisions in earlier matching features.
- Do not expose hidden graph truth to inference.
- Synthetic label signatures cannot appear in filenames, IDs, alias lookups, or feature columns.
- Strong reference-derived silver labels can train a model, but evaluating on the same rule-generated labels only measures agreement with that rule.
- Public BPI trace compliance is not a labeled duplicate-charge benchmark.
- FATURA layout split must test unseen templates where claiming layout generalization; colored/white variants of the same invoice stay together.
- Freeze threshold/calibration before test evaluation.
- No “fine-tuned model” claim for pretrained embeddings plus a newly trained XGBoost classifier; name each correctly.

### 18.6 Error analysis

Save an evaluation table containing:

```text
case/episode ID, relation, source class
true label, prediction, confidence kind/value
chosen/alternative candidate features
wrong amount impact, action consequence
error family: alias | specs | timing | recurring | partial | currency | extraction | coverage
```

Review false positive matches first: a wrong settlement can make an unpaid invoice appear paid.

Bootstrap confidence intervals by episode, not independent pairs, because pairs in one episode are correlated. Amount-weighted metrics must never combine currencies without declared conversion.

### 18.7 Model artifact contract

Store:

```text
model ID/version, task/relation, feature schema
artifact checksum, base encoder revision, dependency lock
training dataset/source manifest, splits, generator version
hyperparameters, calibration method/version, thresholds
metrics by source/split/cohort, limits, intended use
code revision, createdAt, approvedForAutoLink boolean
```

Only load trusted locally generated/verified model artifacts. Python pickle/joblib can execute code; never deserialize a user upload as a model.

### 18.8 Feedback and active learning: stretch

- Store accept/reject/correct-link feedback with evidence and source versions.
- Prioritize low-margin/high-value pairs for human labels.
- Keep reviewers' decision authority separate from model-training label quality.
- Batch retrain on versioned data, reevaluate holdouts, and require explicit artifact promotion.
- Do not update weights online immediately from one chat interaction.
- Monitor confidence/calibration drift by vendor/source/type when real data exists.

### 22.3 Property-based / metamorphic tests

These invariants are more valuable than a few screenshots:

1. Reordering source rows does not change balances.
2. Reimporting identical source bytes does not change cash/liability.
3. Splitting one legitimate settled payment into two referenced transfers of equal sum does not change outstanding.
4. Consolidating referenced payments preserves invoice allocations when remittance amounts are explicit.
5. Splitting a receipt into two with equal sum preserves support.
6. Adding an unrelated company/vendor document does not change this case.
7. Adding a pending payment does not change settled balance.
8. Adding and reversing a payment returns to the prior settlement state.
9. Increasing physical receipt does not fabricate an invoice.
10. Adding an unreferenced ambiguous payment cannot become confirmed settlement without evidence/review.
11. Replacing an explicit PO revision is not additive to original order quantity.
12. Moving an observation timestamp later removes that evidence from earlier knowledge-time replay.
13. No allocation exceeds eligible receipt/cash capacity.
14. Weak transitive duplicate similarities do not automatically merge incoherent invoices.
15. What-if changes do not alter the real case/input records.

### 24.2 Case grouping policy

A procurement episode/case is not a single bank transaction.

- Start from PO and related confirmed source references.
- Support invoices covering multiple PO lines/orders via explicit line allocations, not header-only merging.
- Unreferenced documents can open an unresolved case linked to candidate episodes.
- Payment covering several invoices belongs to payment allocation view across cases; per-case cash uses only allocations, not the full shared transfer.
- Case merge/split requires versioned decision and dependency recalculation.
- Supplier history aggregates distinct obligations/allocations, not repeated case copies.

### 24.3 Threshold, policy, and evidence invalidation

- Changing alias/UOM/policy/model may change derived links/findings; create a new snapshot.
- Reviewer lock applies to specific source revisions and scope.
- If source amount/model/identity changes, revalidate the lock and surface conflict; do not blindly reuse it.
- Policy updates are effective-dated. Replay uses the policy applicable/known under its chosen interpretation, with that interpretation recorded.
- Unreleased hold remains visible even if a model changes; release requires policy/authority and a recorded action.

### 24.4 Accounting support amount detail

Receipt-supported eligible money is calculated from supported line quantities and approved net prices, plus only explicitly validated associated tax/charges under policy.

- Do not multiply a whole invoice gross total by received percentage if lines have different tax/price/charge treatment.
- Header freight/allowances must have an allocation policy and evidence.
- Unknown tax/charge allocation means eligible gross amount may be incomplete; report eligible net plus unresolved components.
- Partial approval is allowed only by configured company policy.
- Booked liability may remain on ERP ledger even while a supported portion is held for clarification.

### 26.3 Final plain-language summary

We are building **Satya**, a digital investigation desk for purchasing and finance teams.

The company imports its orders, warehouse receipts, supplier invoices, credits, and bank transfers. Satya links imperfect records, accounts for split deliveries/payments, separates ordinary timing differences from suspicious or costly contradictions, and calculates what is recorded, what is supported, what is settled, and what remains uncertain.

The user can inspect the exact evidence, understand confidence and alternatives, choose Approve/Request Clarification/Escalate/Place on Hold under company controls, replay earlier knowledge, and preview how new evidence would resolve the case.

A Neo4j graph makes the relationship trail visible. The existing chatbot becomes a company-scoped assistant that retrieves stored facts, analytics, history, and graph paths; it does not invent or calculate financial truth.

Real public procurement traces and public document benchmarks support appropriate components. The equipment business is constructed transparently where public data lacks linked documents/transfers. A trained linkage model and semantic descriptions improve matching, while exact arithmetic, constraints, and versioned business rules protect the final balances.

**The standout story is not “AI says this invoice is wrong.” It is “Here is the evidence, here is the calculation, here is what we do not yet know, and here is the safest next step.”**
